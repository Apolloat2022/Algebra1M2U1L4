Lesson 2 · Key Vocabulary

Key vocabulary

Slope-intercept form

y = mx + b. Shows the slope m and y-intercept (0, b).

y = 2x + 3

Point-slope form

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Shows the slope and a point.

y − 4 = 3(x + 2)

Standard form

Ax + By = C, with integers A, B, C.

6x + 3y = 12

x-intercept

Where the line crosses the x-axis. Put 0 in for y.

(5, 0)

y-intercept

Where the line crosses the y-axis. Put 0 in for x.

(0, −2)

Literal equation

A formula with letters for measures. You can solve it for any letter.

V = Bh → h = V/B

Day 1 · Getting Started · Perimeter Perspectives · SE p. 358 · ★ Essential

Two backyards share sides with a park

The city gives Juan and Mia the perimeter P of the park. Juan needs the width w; Mia needs the length ℓ.

Juan: w = P/2 − ℓ

Mia: ℓ = P/2 − w

2. Show they are equivalent

Multiply each by 2: 2w = P − 2ℓ and 2ℓ = P − 2w

Both become 2ℓ + 2w = P
Getting Started, 15 minutes. Remember: the perimeter of a rectangle is P = 2ℓ + 2w. Same formula, solved for the variable each person needs.

Which form shows what?

FormEquationShows quickly
slope-intercepty = mx + bslope, y-intercept
point-slopey − y₁ = m(x − x₁)slope, a point
standardAx + By = Cslope −A/B, x-int (C/A, 0), y-int (0, C/B)

In standard form A, B, and C are integers, and A and B are not both 0.

Day 2 · Activity 2.2 · Rewriting Literal Equations · SE p. 362 · ★ Essential

Celsius and Fahrenheit

A literal equation is an equation in which the variables represent specific measures, like a formula.

C = 5/9(F − 32)

1. Multiply by 9/5: 9/5 C = F − 32

F = 9/5 C + 32

2. Who is warmer?

Jayden: 77°F. Mateo: 30°C.

F = 9/5(30) + 32 = 86

Mateo: 86°F > 77°F
Activity 2.2, 15–20 minutes. Either convert 30°C to °F (86) or 77°F to °C (25).

Day 2 · Activity 2.3 · Solving Literal Equations for Specific Variables · SE pp. 365–366 · ★ Essential

Solve for the height

1. Box

V = Bh
h = V/B

V = 450, B = 75: h = 6 in.

2. Cone

V = ⅓πr²h
h = 3V/(πr²)

cubic ÷ square units = linear units

3. Trapezoid

A = ½h(b₁ + b₂)
h = 2A/(b₁ + b₂)

A = 24, bases 9 and 7: h = 3 cm

Questions 1–3, 15–20 minutes. Skills Practice Set D.

Day 2 · Activity 2.3 · Question 4 · SE p. 366 · ★ Essential

Solve Z = A/B + C/D for each variable

a. Solve for A

Multiply by B: BZ = A + BC/D

A = BZ − BC/D

b. Solve for D (challenge)

Multiply by BD: BDZ = AD + BC

Factor out D: D(BZ − A) = BC

D = BC/(BZ − A)
Part b is a challenge: collect the D terms on one side and factor out D.

Day 2 · Talk the Talk · Rearrange It · SE p. 367

Cans of soup (use 3.14 for π)

1. Lateral area S = 2πrh. Solve for h.

h = S/(2πr)

2. S = 37.68, r = 1.5.

h = 4 in.

3. Surface area S = 2πr² + 2πrh. Solve for h.

h = (S − 2πr²)/(2πr)

4. S = 51.81, r = 1.5.

Essential Question · Answered

How do you solve for one variable in a formula?

Use the same properties of equality: undo each operation on that variable, doing the same to both sides.

Every other letter is treated like a number.

Pick the form that shows what you need: slope-intercept for the slope and y-intercept, point-slope for a point.

Have students answer the Essential Question on the lesson opener page.
