Key vocabulary

Getting Started · Goats, Chickens, and Pigs · SE p. 412

Is the third trade fair?

Trade 1: 4 goats = 5 chickens

Trade 2: 1 pig = 2 chickens + 1 goat

Trade 3: Lyndi's 3 chickens + 1 pig against Simpson's 4 goats.

Replace the pig: 3C + (2C + G) = 5C + G

Replace the goats: 4G = 5C

Lyndi's side is worth 1 goat more. Simpson adds 1 goat to make it fair.
Not starred. This is substitution without the name: swap something for what it equals.

Activity 2.1 · Introduction to Substitution · SE p. 413 · ★ Essential

Sofia's $30 potato salad

Onions $1.25/lb, potatoes $1.05/lb: 1.25x + 1.05y = 30

8 times as many pounds of potatoes: y = 8x

Does (1, 8) work? 1.25 + 8.40 = 9.65 ≠ 30: no.

Questions 1–6. The graph only gives an estimate; the exact point is hard to read. That is why we need an algebraic method.

Activity 2.1 · Worked Example · SE p. 415 · ★ Essential

The substitution method

Step 1 Pick an equation with a variable alone: y = 8x

Step 2 Substitute 8x for y: 1.25x + 1.05(8x) = 30

Step 3 Solve: 9.65x = 30, so x ≈ 3.1

Then y = 8(3.1) = 24.8

Step 4 Check in both equations:

1.25(3.1) + 1.05(24.8) ≈ 30 ✓

About 3.1 lb of onions and 24.8 lb of potatoes
Questions 7–9. The substitution method replaces a variable in one equation with an equivalent expression from the other equation.

Activity 2.2 · Substitution with Special Systems · SE pp. 417–419 · ★ Essential

Daniel and Chloe earn $7 an hour

Daniel starts at 7:00: y = 7x

Chloe starts 90 minutes later: y = 7(x − 1.5) = 7x − 10.5

By noon: Daniel $35, Chloe $24.50.

Substitute: 7x = 7x − 10.5

0 = −10.5

False: no solution. Parallel lines; Chloe never catches up.
Questions 1–9. When the variable disappears and the statement is false, the system has no solution.

Activity 2.2 · Same Line · SE pp. 419–420 · ★ Essential

$4 tickets, 5 tickets, $20

4x + 4y = 20 → y = −x + 5

x + y = 5 → y = −x + 5

Substitute: −x + 5 = −x + 5

5 = 5

Always true: infinite solutions. Any 5 tickets cost $20.
Questions 10–16. When the variable disappears and the statement is true, the two equations are the same line.

Activity 2.3 · Writing and Solving Systems · SE pp. 421–422 · ★ Essential

Band tickets: $236,000 from 4000 seats

2800 main-level seats at x dollars, 1200 upper-level at y dollars.

2800x + 1200y = 236000

Main level costs $20 more: x = y + 20

2800(y + 20) + 1200y = 236000

4000y + 56000 = 236000 → y = 45

Main level $65, upper level $45
Questions 1–4. Question 1 asks only for the system. Check: 2800(65) + 1200(45) = 182,000 + 54,000 = 236,000.

Essential Question · Answered

If the intersection is not clear from a graph, how can you determine the solution to a system?

Solve one equation for a variable, substitute that expression into the other equation, solve, then substitute back.

A false statement (like 0 = −10.5) means no solution. A true statement (like 5 = 5) means infinite solutions.

Have students answer the Essential Question on the lesson opener page.
