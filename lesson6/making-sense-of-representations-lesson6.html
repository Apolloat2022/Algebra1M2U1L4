Students decide whether a table, graph, equation, or scenario is linear, write functions from tables, and read meaning from two forms of a linear function: general form f(x) = ax + b and factored form f(x) = a(x − c). Day 1 · Getting Started · Well, Are Ya or Aren't Ya? · SE p. 240

Linear or nonlinear? Explain your reasoning.

Scenario A

A tree grows 3.5 inches each year.

Linear. Same change, 3.5 in, every year.

Scenario B

The strength of a medication decreases by 50% each hour it is in the patient's system.

Nonlinear. Half of a smaller amount each hour, so the drop shrinks.

Scenario C

The area of a square depends on its side length.

Nonlinear. A = s2: side 1, 2, 3 gives area 1, 4, 9.

Equation A

y = 14 − 9x

Linear. x has exponent 1; slope −9.

Equation B

y = 2x + 1

Nonlinear. The variable is in the exponent.

Equation C

y = {{1|4}}(x + 7) − 1

Linear. Point-slope structure; slope {{1|4}}.

Getting Started, 10–15 minutes. Have pairs complete Question 1 before you reveal anything, then share as a class. Teacher-guide misconception: students may think that because they know a formula for a relationship, like Scenario C, the relationship must be linear. Have them use the formula to make a table (side 1, 2, 3 → area 1, 4, 9; the differences are 3 and 5, not constant). Ask: What is another way to describe "decreases by 50% each hour"? (Multiplied by one-half each hour.) Which feature identifies whether an equation is a linear function? (The variable's exponent is 1 and it isn't in an exponent.)

Day 1 · Activity 6.1 · Summary

Is the table linear?

1. Check the inputs. If they're not consecutive (or not in order), first differences won't work.

2. Calculate the slope, {{change in y|change in x}}, between every pair of points.

3. If the slope is always the same, the table is linear. Use the slope and a point to find b.

4. It's a direct variation only if it passes through (0, 0): y = kx.

Stamp the learning, from the teacher guide: "If a table represents a linear function, the slope, or average rate of change, is constant between all given points." To close Day 1, have students reread the Essential Question and read the activity summaries to the class. End of Day 1.

Write and evaluate E(t)

Day 2 · Activity 6.3 · Worked Example · SE p. 248

Polynomials

A polynomial is a sum of powers of a variable multiplied by coefficients. A linear function is a polynomial function.

The degree is the greatest exponent of the variable.

The leading coefficient is the number multiplying the term with the greatest power.

When you graph a polynomial, the degree tells you the maximum number of times the graph can cross the x-axis.

NameExampleDegree
ConstantP(x) = 70
LinearP(x) = 2x − 51
QuadraticP(x) = 3x2 − 2x + 42
CubicP(x) = 4x3 − 23
Ask a student to read the definitions aloud. "Polynomial" comes from poly- (many) and -nomial (term): many terms. Teacher-guide questions: Rewrite the constant 7 with a variable and an exponent (7x⁰). What is the exponent of the variable in a linear function? (1.) What is an example of a linear function with a leading coefficient of 1? (P(x) = x + 4.) Write an example of a cubic function with four terms (x³ + 2x² − x + 5). The leading coefficient of 3x² − 2x + 4 is 3. Students learn more about polynomials in later courses.

Day 2 · Activity 6.3 · Question 5 · SE pp. 248–249

General form: f(x) = ax + b

a and b are real numbers and a ≠ 0. It has the same structure as y = mx + b.

a is the leading coefficient: the steepness and direction of the line (the slope).

b is the y-intercept.

5. For G(t) = 15t − 45, label a and b on the graph.

a = 15: a slope triangle, right 2 and up 30.

b = −45: the y-intercept, (0, −45).

Read the information about general form aloud, then complete Question 5 as a class. The degree of a linear polynomial is 1, so its graph crosses the x-axis at most once. Teacher-guide sidebar: the letters used are irrelevant; think about the position of each number, as the leading coefficient or as the constant, and its effect on the function. The dashed slope triangle on the graph goes from (5, 30), right 2 and up 30 to (7, 60): 30/2 = 15. The last click marks b. Ask: Explain how your label for a makes sense. Where is another possible location for a? (Any slope triangle on the line.)

Day 2 · Activity 6.3 · Questions 6–7 · SE p. 249

Factored form: f(x) = a(x − c)

a is the slope. The value of x that makes (x − c) = 0 is the x-intercept.

A zero of a function is a real number that makes f(x) = 0.

6. For G(t) = 15(t − 3), label a and c.

a = 15: the slope triangle. c = 3: the x-intercept, (3, 0).

7. What is the zero of G(t)?

3, because 15(3 − 3) = 0

A zero is a number (3). An intercept is a point (3, 0).

Read the definition of zero of a function aloud, then have pairs complete Questions 6 and 7. Teacher-guide misconception: students mix up whether to give an x-intercept or a zero as one number or an ordered pair. Intercepts are locations, written as ordered pairs like (3, 0). Zeros are the values where f(x) = 0, written as numbers like 3. Ask: What information do general form and factored form both give? (The slope, a.) What does each give that the other doesn't? (General: the y-intercept. Factored: the zero.) Stamp the learning: the general form and the factored form each have a structure that identifies characteristics of the graph. For more practice, assign Skills Practice Set C. To close Day 2, have students reread the Essential Question and read the activity summaries. End of Day 2.

Day 3 · Activity 6.4 · Question 2 · SE p. 251

Three ways to write F(t)

a. Write F(t) in terms of G(t).

F(t) = G(t) − 35

b. General form. What do a and b show?

F(t) = 15t − 80

a = 15: slope triangle. b = −80: y-intercept (0, −80).

c. Factored form, with c as a fraction. What do a and c show?

F(t) = 15(t − {{16|3}})

a = 15: slope triangle. c = {{16|3}}: x-intercept (5{{1|3}}, 0).

Work: G(t) − 35 = 15t − 45 − 35 = 15t − 80. To factor, divide 80 by 15: 15t − 80 = 15(t − 80/15) = 15(t − 16/3). Check: 15 × 16/3 = 80. Ask: How can you rewrite general form to get factored form? (Factor out the leading coefficient.) How can you use what you know about factored form to find the factor (t − 16/3)? (Set F(t) = 0 and solve.) The zero 16/3 ≈ 5.33 matches 1c: she needs 6 shirts before she has any money. Stamp the learning: in general form f(x) = ax + b, a is the slope and b is the y-intercept; in factored form f(x) = a(x − c), a is the slope and c is the x-intercept, or zero.

Essential Question · Answered

How can the structure of the equation tell you about the function?

General form f(x) = ax + b: a is the leading coefficient, the steepness and direction; b is the y-intercept.

Factored form f(x) = a(x − c): a is the slope; c is the zero, and (c, 0) is the x-intercept.

A linear function is a polynomial of degree 1, so its graph crosses the x-axis at most once.

Have students answer the Essential Question on the lesson opener page. This is the teacher guide's sample answer: "The general form of a linear function is f(x) = ax + b, where a and b are real numbers and a ≠ 0. In this form, the a-value is the leading coefficient that describes the steepness and direction of the line. The b-value describes the y-intercept. The factored form of a linear function is f(x) = a(x − c), where a and c are real numbers and a ≠ 0. In this form, the a-value is the slope and the value of x that makes the factor (x − c) equal to zero is the x-intercept."
