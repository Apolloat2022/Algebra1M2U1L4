Graphing Linear Equations

I can…

✓  graph a line from y = mx + b using the y-intercept and the slope.

✓  graph a line from point-slope form.

✓  find the intercepts of Ax + By = C and graph the line.

Day 1 · Bell Work · Review

Warm up: work these on your own

1. Slope and y-intercept of y = {{1|2}}x − 3?

m = {{1|2}}, (0, −3)

2. Slope and a point on y − 4 = −2(x + 1)?

m = −2, (−1, 4)

3. Solve for y: 4x + 2y = 10

y = −2x + 5

4. Start at (0, 1). Go up 3 and right 2. Where do you land?

(2, 4)
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). These review what today builds on: reading the slope and y-intercept from slope-intercept form, reading the slope and point from point-slope form (Lesson 4), solving for y (the Lesson 4 Prepare page), and counting rise over run on a grid. Reveal the answers one at a time. Watch #2: y − 4 = −2(x + 1) means the point is (−1, 4), because the sign inside the parentheses flips. Answer key: 1. y = ½x − 3: m = ½, y-intercept (0, −3). 2. y − 4 = −2(x + 1): m = −2, point (−1, 4). 3. 4x + 2y = 10: 2y = −4x + 10, so y = −2x + 5. 4. From (0, 1), up 3 and right 2 lands on (2, 4).

Day 2 · Bell Work · Review of Day 1

Warm up: work these on your own

1. Name the y-intercept and two more points on y = −{{1|3}}x + 4. (SE p. 220)

(0, 4), (3, 3), (−3, 5)

2. Lena has $25 and saves $5 a week. Write an equation. (SE p. 221)

y = 5x + 25

3. Slope and a point on y + 2 = 4(x − 1)? (Lesson 4)

m = 4, (1, −2)

4. Where does y = 2x − 6 cross the x-axis?

(3, 0)
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). #1 and #2 review Day 1. #3 reviews reading point-slope form, which Activity 5.2 uses to graph. #4 previews the x-intercept from Activity 5.3: on the x-axis, y is 0. Answer key: 1. y = −⅓x + 4: y-intercept (0, 4); down 1, right 3 gives (3, 3); up 1, left 3 gives (−3, 5). 2. y = 5x + 25 (x = weeks, y = dollars saved). 3. y + 2 = 4(x − 1): m = 4, point (1, −2). 4. Set y = 0: 0 = 2x − 6, so x = 3; the point is (3, 0).

Day 3 · Bell Work · Review of Day 2

Warm up: work these on your own

1. x- and y-intercepts of 2x + 5y = 20? (SE p. 227)

(10, 0) and (0, 4)

2. Slope of 3x − 4y = 12? (SE p. 228)

m = {{3|4}}

3. Write y = {{2|3}}x − 1 in standard form. (SE p. 230)

2x − 3y = 3

4. Standard form through (0, 6) and (3, 0)? (SE p. 230)

2x + y = 6
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). It reviews Day 2. Watch #2: the slope is −A/B = −3/(−4) = 3/4; students who forget the sign of B get −3/4. Answer key: 1. y = 0 gives 2x = 20, x = 10; x = 0 gives 5y = 20, y = 4. Intercepts (10, 0) and (0, 4). 2. m = −A/B = −3/(−4) = 3/4. 3. Multiply by 3: 3y = 2x − 3, so −2x + 3y = −3, or 2x − 3y = 3. 4. m = (0 − 6)/(3 − 0) = −2, so y = −2x + 6, and 2x + y = 6.

Lesson 5 · Vocabulary

Words we need

y-intercept

Where the line crosses the y-axis: (0, b).

y = 2x + 5 → (0, 5)

Rise over run

Rise: up (+) or down (−). Run: right.

m = {{3|2}}: up 3, right 2

x-intercept

Where the line crosses the x-axis: (x, 0).

Put 0 in for y.

Standard form

Ax + By = C

3x + 2y = 12

Slope-intercept form

m is the slope, b is the y-intercept.

y = mx + b

Point-slope form

Slope and one point.

y − y1 = m(x − x1)

Lesson 5 · Steps

How to graph y = mx + b

1. Plot the y-intercept, (0, b).

2. Write the slope as {{rise|run}}.

3. From the point, count the rise (up or down), then the run (right). Plot a point.

4. Do it again, so you have 3 points. Connect them with a ruler.

Leave this slide up, or give students a printed copy, while they work.

I do · Watch me

Graph y = −2x + 10

Step 1: b = 10. Plot (0, 10).

Step 2: m = {{−2|1}}: down 2, right 1.

(1, 8) and (2, 6)

Step 3: connect with a ruler.

Point to each point as it appears. Negative slope: the line falls left to right.

x-intercept: put y = 0.

(4, 0)

(0, 6)

Lesson 5 · Which form?

Where do I start?

y = mx + b

Start at (0, b). Count the slope.

Point-slope

Start at (x1, y1). Count the slope.

Ax + By = C

Find both intercepts. Connect them.
