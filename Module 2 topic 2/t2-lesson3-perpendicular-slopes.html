Students rotate a segment 90° at a time, prove that perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes, work with horizontal and vertical lines, and write equations of perpendicular lines. Two days on the 150-day calendar: Day 1 Getting Started and Activity 3.1; Day 2 Activities 3.2, 3.3 and Talk the Talk. Materials: patty paper, straightedges.

Day 1 · Bell Work · Review

Warm up: work these on your own

1. What is the reciprocal of ⅔?

3/2

2. Rotate the point (8, 5) 90° counterclockwise about the origin. Where does it go? (SE p. 306)

(−5, 8)

3. What is the slope of the segment from (0, 0) to (8, 5)? (SE p. 306)

5/8

4. What is the slope of y = −3x + 5?

−3
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). The same 4 questions are on the online Day 1 bell work. Reveal the answers one at a time. Answer key: 1. Flip the fraction: ⅔ · 3/2 = 1. 2. A 90° counterclockwise rotation maps (x, y) to (−y, x). 3. Slope = (5 − 0) ÷ (8 − 0) = 5/8. 4. In y = mx + b, m = −3.

Essential Question · SE p. 305

You have translated graphs of linear equations to find the slopes of parallel lines.

How can you rotate the graphs of linear equations to determine the slopes of perpendicular lines?

Have students write the Essential Question at the top of their notes. Don't answer it yet; we come back to it at the end of the lesson.

Day 1 · Getting Started · Coordinate Rotation · SE p. 306

Rotate AB, A(0, 0) and B(8, 5), about the origin

Original90°180°270°
A(0, 0)(0, 0)(0, 0)(0, 0)
B(8, 5)(−5, 8)(−8, −5)(5, −8)

Each 90° counterclockwise turn maps (x, y) to (−y, x). A is the center, so it stays.

Slopes: AB and A″B″: 5/8. A′B′ and A‴B‴: −8/5.

The two slopes are reciprocals with opposite signs: (5/8)(−8/5) = −1.

Getting Started, 15–20 minutes. Patty paper helps: trace the segment and the axes, then turn the paper a quarter turn.

Day 1 · Activity 3.1 · Slopes of Perpendicular Lines · SE p. 307

A line and its 90° rotation

Pair A

y = x
y = −x

Pair B

y = ⅓x + 5
y = −3x + 5

Pair C

y = −⅔x + 9
y = 3/2x − 4

Each line is rotated 90°, so the lines meet at a right angle: they are perpendicular.

The slopes of each pair are reciprocals with opposite signs.

Activity 3.1, 20–25 minutes. Students read the three graphs in their book (SE p. 307) and write the equations.

Day 1 · Activity 3.1 · Worked Example · SE p. 308

Proof: perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals

Given p ⊥ q. Point R(a, b) is on p.

Rotate R 90° counterclockwise about O: it lands on q at D(−b, a).

Slope of p: m₁ = b/a. Slope of q: m₂ = a/(−b).

−1/m₂ = −1 · (−b/a) = b/a = m₁

So m₁ = −1/m₂, and m₁ · m₂ = −1.

Have students restate each step. Question 3 asks for the same proof with a clockwise rotation: R(a, b) maps to D(b, −a), so m₂ = −a/b and again m₁ = −1/m₂.

Day 1 · Activity 3.1 · Question 4 · SE p. 309

Line j ⊥ line k. Find the slope of line k.

a.  m = ⅔
m = −3/2
b.  m = −⅘
m = 5/4
c.  m = −3
m = ⅓

Flip the fraction and change the sign. Check: the product is −1.

Stamp the learning: perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals. End of Day 1.

Reveal the answers one at a time. Answer key: 1. Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals: ⅔ · (−3/2) = −1. 2. On a horizontal line y never changes, so the rise is 0. 3. Day 2 · Activity 3.2 · Horizontal and Vertical Lines · SE pp. 310–311

Extend the segments into lines

Line p is vertical: x = 4. Lines q, r and s are horizontal: y = 4, y = 8, y = 12.

Horizontal

When x goes up 1, y doesn't change. Slope = 0/(x₂ − x₁) = 0.

Vertical

When y goes up 1, x doesn't change. Slope = (y₂ − y₁)/0: undefined.

Activity 3.2, 15–20 minutes. Memory trick from Topic 1: HOY VUX.

Day 2 · Talk the Talk · Things Aren't Always What They Seem · SE p. 314

Linh or Jackson?

Linh says lines a, b and c are parallel to each other and perpendicular to lines d, e and f. Jackson says they are parallel but not perpendicular to d, e, f.

Jackson is correct. The axes use different scales.

Lines a, b, c have slope 1, so they are parallel. Lines d, e, f have slope −¼.

1 · (−¼) ≠ −1: the slopes are not negative reciprocals, so the lines are not perpendicular.

Show the graph in the book (SE p. 314). The x-axis counts 1 per square and the y-axis 1 per 2 squares, so the lines look perpendicular but aren't.

Essential Question · Answered

How does rotation give perpendicular slopes?

A 90° rotation maps (x, y) to (−y, x), so a slope b/a becomes −a/b.

Perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes: their product is −1.

Any vertical line is perpendicular to any horizontal line.

Have students answer the Essential Question on the lesson opener page.
