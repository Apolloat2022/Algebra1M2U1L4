Students compare linear functions given as tables, graphs, equations and verbal descriptions: slope (parallel or perpendicular), y-intercept, and the units and scales in context. The 150-day guide omits this lesson, but the End of Topic 2 Assessment covers it (questions 11–14). On the class calendar: Day 1 (Mon Nov 2, review day) Getting Started, Activities 4.1 and 4.2, then the online Topic 2 Review practice test; Day 2 (Wed Nov 4, after the test, for students not retaking) Activities 4.3, 4.4 and Talk the Talk.

Day 1 · Bell Work · Review

Warm up: work these on your own

1. What is the slope of the line through (−2.5, −6) and (0, 4)? (SE p. 321)

4

2. What slope does a line parallel to y = 4x − 3 have? (SE p. 321)

4

3. What slope does a line perpendicular to y = 4x − 3 have? (SE p. 321)

−¼

4. Function p passes through (0, 1) and (4, 4). Which function has a greater rate of change than function p? (SE p. 325)

y = x − 2
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). The same 4 questions are on the online Day 1 bell work. Reveal the answers one at a time. Answer key: 1. Slope = (4 − (−6)) ÷ (0 − (−2.5)) = 10 ÷ 2.5 = 4. 2. Parallel lines have equal slopes. 3. The negative reciprocal of 4 is −¼. 4. p has slope (4 − 1) ÷ 4 = ¾ = 0.75. y = x − 2 has slope 1, which is greater. (3x − 4y = 8 is y = ¾x − 2: equal, not greater.)

Lesson 4 · SE p. 319

Today you will…

1. Compare linear functions represented algebraically, graphically, in tables, or with verbal descriptions.

2. Choose and interpret appropriate units to represent independent and dependent quantities in situations modeled by linear functions.

3. Choose and interpret appropriate scales and origins for graphs of linear functions.

Day 2 · Bell Work · Review of Day 1

Warm up: work these on your own

1. Travis County pays $15 per ton of cardboard. How much is paid for 3 tons? (SE p. 322)

$45

2. Hays County's graph passes through (0, 0) and (1, 20). What is the payout per ton? (SE p. 322)

$20

3. J(x) = 9/5x + 32 converts °C to °F. What is J(10)? (SE p. 323)

50

4. A 200-gallon tank drains 10 gallons each minute: w(t) = 200 − 10t. What is the range of the function for this situation? (SE p. 325)

0 ≤ w(t) ≤ 200
Added for this deck; not from the textbook. Have this up as students walk in; they work silently while you take attendance (about 5 minutes). The same 4 questions are on the online Day 2 bell work. Reveal the answers one at a time. Answer key: 1. 15 · 3 = 45. 2. Slope = 20 ÷ 1 = 20 dollars per ton. 3. 9/5 · 10 + 32 = 18 + 32 = 50, so 10°C is 50°F. 4. Range = the outputs, the gallons left: 200 at the start, down to 0 when t = 20.

Slope: Joey's is about 5/9 (each 9°F is about 5°C). Xavier's is 9/5 (each 5°C is 9°F).

Essential Question · Answered

How do the representations compare?

Every form (equation, table, graph, description) shows the same features: the slope or rate and the y-intercept or starting value.

Compare by finding these in each form: equal slopes are parallel; slopes with a product of −1 are perpendicular.

The quantities and units tell what the slope and y-intercept mean.
